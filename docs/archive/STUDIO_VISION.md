# Bzor Computational Studio — vision and operating model

## Purpose

Build a public experimental studio where KC Austin and an AI collaborator discover
computational behaviours, develop reusable visual languages, find revealing viewpoints,
and make finished audiovisual specimens in Houdini.

The studio exists to produce work KC wants to see in the world. Public attention is a
useful consequence and distribution problem, not the creative brief.

The working identity remains **computational natural history**: observations and artifacts
from plausible alternate natures. The scope expands from individual simulations to the
complete practice that discovers, interprets, presents, and publishes them.

## Central rules

1. Discover behaviours cheaply.
2. Separate behavioural discovery from visual polish.
3. Develop looks, colour systems, and cinematography as independent practices.
4. Combine components only when the pairing reveals something neither contains alone.
5. Preserve meaningful failures and disagreements, not every generated variation.
6. Promote by artist judgment, supported by evidence rather than automatic scores.
7. Publish exhaust from real work; do not turn the laboratory into a content mill.
8. KC has final creative authority. The AI should propose boldly, criticize directly,
   explain its evidence, and follow firm direction.
9. Public actions always require explicit approval.

## Studio tracks

### Behavior Lab — what happens?

The Behavior Lab implements minimal local systems and asks whether their consequences are
surprising, legible, and worth returning to. Sources include research papers, natural
processes, artificial life, cellular systems, fields, agent models, and speculative rules.

A paper reproduction is first implemented faithfully enough to understand and verify its
mechanism. It is then deliberately mutated. Reproduction, interpretation, and original
mutation must remain clearly labelled.

Typical outputs:

- cheap fixed-camera diagnostic loops;
- VEX or Python kernels and reproducible HIP/HIPLC scenes;
- instrument views, metrics, force decompositions, and field slices;
- bounded parameter families and selected anomalies;
- concise observations, hypotheses, failures, and next mutations;
- promotion candidates.

A behaviour does not need expensive rendering to earn attention. If polish is required to
make it interesting, it remains a look test rather than a promoted behaviour.

### Look Development Lab — what can it become?

The Look Development Lab develops physical and representational languages independently
of a particular simulation: MaterialX families, Vellum structures, fibers, membranes,
instances, surfaces, VDBs, fluids, particles, lighting rigs, compositing treatments, and
procedural sound mappings.

Look studies use canonical test scenes and cheap caches. A setup becomes a reusable
component only after succeeding in at least two materially different contexts. This keeps
the project from building generalized assets before their actual shape is understood.

### Colour within artist-led Look — how does colour carry meaning?

Colour remains an important creative concern, but it is authored by KC in the Look HIP rather than
promoted through a separate Study phase or directory.

Colour is represented as a semantic system rather than a row of swatches. Palette roles
include `field`, `body`, `secondary`, `signal`, `trace`, `instrument`, and `void`.
Each accepted palette records perceptual OKLCH colours, intended proportions, value
hierarchy, lighting context, gamut, and any behavioural or temporal mappings.

Supported grammars include monochromatic, analogous, complementary, split-complementary,
triadic, tetradic, warm/cool, neutral-plus-signal, and deliberate discordance. Ratio
families such as 80/15/5, 60/30/10, 45/45/10, 70/20/8/2, and 90/9/1 are starting points,
not laws. Evaluation considers luminance, chroma, area, placement, and material response.

Three related layers stay distinct:

- **Instrument colour:** stable semantics and maximum diagnostic clarity.
- **Specimen colour:** expressive, artwork-specific colour.
- **Field Station colour:** consistent interface and publication identity.

Consistency should come from relationships and constraints, not a compulsory accent colour.
The emerging house language favours controlled fields, strong value hierarchy, hued darks,
and scarce high-chroma signals. Deliberate violations are welcome when recorded as intent.

### Camera and framing within artist-led Look — how should it be seen?

Camera and framing remain in the artist-owned Look HIP rather than a separate Cinematography phase.

The camera interprets behaviour. It may reveal scale, follow an event, isolate a local
interaction, conceal information, or create ambiguity. It should not merely record the
centre of a bounding box.

Every candidate shot may describe:

- primary and counter-subjects;
- foreground, midground, and background roles;
- protected negative space and intended eye path;
- dominant motion, frame entry, exit, silhouette, and depth hierarchy;
- focus target and focus policy;
- lens, aspect ratio, camera behaviour, and temporal event window;
- compositional guides such as thirds, diagonals, symmetry, golden sections, lead room,
  frames within frames, and vanishing structures.

Metrics identify broken or potentially interesting candidates; they never declare good art.
Camera scouts optimize for useful diversity: static observer, macro witness, frontier
follow, cohort camera, event camera, field camera, inside observer, sectional observer,
orbital survey, locked architecture, and adversarial viewpoints.

Temporal scouting looks for density changes, mergers, division, saturation, extinction,
regrowth, symmetry breaking, unusual state transitions, and peaks in curvature or disorder.
The result is a small coverage set: instrument, establishing, intimate, structural, and
interpretive views.

### Specimen Studio — what deserves completion?

The Specimen Studio receives the locked Look render and combines it with KC's detail/overlay pass,
sound, editorial treatment, and final packaging into a finished audiovisual work.

A specimen normally produces:

- a sharp instrument version;
- one or more authored organism shots;
- archival and platform media;
- sound or an explicit decision to remain silent;
- a reproducible source package;
- a field note with lineage, credits, dependencies, and accessibility text.

### Field Station — what is preserved and shared?

The Field Station is the canonical public archive. It should expose a living specimen
index rather than imitate a generic science-fiction dashboard.

Study pages may contain:

- observation, question, and source references;
- mechanism, reproduction notes, and original mutations;
- behaviour loops, metrics, and instrument views;
- selected failures and competing hypotheses;
- parameters, source excerpts, seeds, dependencies, and downloadable HIP files;
- look, palette, camera, and sound pairings;
- final specimens, descendants, and branch lineage;
- clearly separated measured, derived, observed, and hypothesized claims.

The interface should make branches, promotions, relationships, and temporal development
visible. The same structured records generate publication packages without exposing private
notes by default.

## Promotion model

Promotion is explicit and reversible:

```text
inbox idea
→ scoped proposal
→ probe
→ observed candidate
→ promoted component
→ specimen candidate
→ approved specimen
→ published field note
```

Tracks have their own promoted components. A behaviour can be promoted without a look; a
palette or camera rig can be promoted without a behaviour. A specimen references exact
versions of the components it combines.

Promotion criteria:

- **Behaviour:** surprising consequence, return value, legibility, or conceptual charge.
- **Look:** distinctive material or representational language with useful extension points.
- **Palette:** coherent hierarchy, role clarity, and useful behaviour under actual lighting.
- **Shot:** reveals or productively reframes the system across time.
- **Specimen:** the combination has emotional or conceptual force and is technically sound.

Scores and metrics are supporting evidence only. KC's gut judgment is the final gate.
Rejection means `archive`, `mutate`, or `hold`; it does not imply failure.

## Public rhythm

- **Website / Field Station:** complete durable archive and canonical source.
- **X:** field observations, paper implementations, theories, failures, short behaviour
  clips, branch questions, and links to full notes.
- **Instagram:** polished specimens and occasional behaviour-to-specimen carousels.
- **YouTube:** final films, longer field reports, research-to-Houdini explorations, and
  related-study collections.

One meaningful experiment may produce several platform adaptations. Platform demand must
not determine how many experiments are made or when a specimen is considered finished.
